MINIMUM_PAYMENTS (313 missing) filled with 0. In the sample data, the customer with a missing value (C10004) also has PAYMENTS = 0. The likely cause is that no payment was made, so there was no minimum payment recorded. Filling with 0 keeps that meaning and avoids inventing values.

CREDIT_LIMIT (1 missing) filled with the median. The median is robust to the skew, and one row cannot affect the result.

Elbow method

I ran K-Means for k = 2 to 10 and plotted inertia. Inertia falls from about 128k (k=2) to 112k (k=3) and 99k (k=4), then declines more gradually. There is no sharp elbow, only a gentle bend around k = 3–4, so the elbow alone cannot fix the number of clusters. I used the silhouette score to decide.

Select the Best Model¶

K-Means (k = 3) achieved a silhouette score of 0.250 versus 0.176 for Agglomerative Clustering. K-Means was therefore selected because it separates the clusters better, is faster on large datasets, and provides centroids that are easy to interpret and can assign new customers. Its other metrics were a Davies-Bouldin index of 1.60 (lower is better) and a Calinski-Harabasz score of 1,605. These indicate moderate but usable cluster structure.

K-Means with k = 3 produced three business-relevant segments: cash-advance users, high-value spenders, and mainstream customers.

Limitations: the silhouette score of 0.25 shows moderate overlap between segments, one cluster holds 68% of customers.

Customers differ mainly in how they use the card (heavy spending vs. reliance on cash advances vs. light everyday use), which is what is needed to recommend the right financial product. The cash-advance group pays its balance in full only 3.4% of the time, which makes it the most important group for lending and risk offers.
